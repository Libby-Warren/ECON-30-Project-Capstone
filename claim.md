# Capstone Claim — The SFR Machine

## Core thesis

After the 2007–2009 housing crisis, institutional investors turned scattered single-family rentals into a **repeatable buy–operate–finance platform**. That form of progress in housing finance creates a progress-and-inequality puzzle: nationally the footprint is still limited, but locally it can be highly visible—and credible evidence on prices, rents, homeownership access, and tenant outcomes is **mixed**.

## Hero hook (one sentence)

Scale in single-family rentals is not just owning more homes; it is running housing like an integrated operating and financing system—with uneven consequences for renters, buyers, and policymakers.

## Central puzzle

If institutional single-family rental (SFR) ownership is a **small share of the national housing stock**, why does it feel so urgent in specific metros and policy debates—and why do government reports, industry analysts, and advocates disagree on whether the model mainly helps or harms communities?

## What this project argues (modest causal scope)

**We argue (descriptive + mechanistic):**

1. Institutional SFR emerged from a specific historical window (foreclosure crisis, tight mortgage credit, rental demand).
2. The competitive advantage is **systems**: acquisition at scale, centralized operations, and access to capital markets—not merely portfolio size.
3. Portfolio composition differs from local investors (e.g., tilt toward newer housing stock), which supports the “standardized platform” story.
4. Policy attention is high because effects may **cluster locally** even when national aggregates look small, and because definitions and data limits make causal claims hard.

**We do not argue:**

- That institutional investors are the primary driver of national housing unaffordability.
- That all large landlords harm tenants or that bans are obviously correct.
- That we can precisely estimate causal effects on rents or homeownership without better microdata.

## Progress and inequality (ECON 30 framing)

- **Progress:** Capital, data, and professional management scaled a fragmented rental asset class.
- **Inequality / distribution:** Who gains access to ownership, who rents formerly owner-occupied homes, and who bears risk when financing conditions change.

## Primary contribution

Explain the **institutional SFR platform** clearly for a non-expert audience, support it with credible quantitative snapshots, and show why policy responses require tradeoffs—not slogans.
